--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. STUDY BACKGROUND & OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Context: Unlike controlled lab evaluations or synthetic benchmarks, this paper 
  investigates how real-world users experience and describe LLM "hallucinations" 
  in the wild.
* Dataset: A large-scale empirical study analyzing 3,000,000 user reviews from 
  90 diverse AI-powered mobile apps across 10 categories (e.g., General Chatbots, 
  Educational AI, Generative Tools) from January 2022 to December 2024.
* Method: A mixed-methods approach using a heuristic-based detection algorithm 
  to flag candidate reviews, followed by manual qualitative annotation (Cohen's 
  Kappa >= 0.75).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. KEY FINDINGS: PREVALENCE & TRUST IMPACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Prevalence Rate: 
  Approximately 1.75% of reviews initially flagged as relevant to AI errors were 
  confirmed to describe user-perceived LLM hallucinations.
* Trust Erosion (The Star Rating Crash):
  - Overall average rating of analyzed apps: 3.9 stars.
  - Average rating of reviews mentioning AI but not hallucinating: 3.5 stars.
  - Average rating of reviews reporting hallucinations: 1.8 stars.
  - Implication: A hallucination is perceived as a critical product defect rather 
    than an algorithmic quirk, causing a severe drop in user rating.
* Category Sensitivity: 
  "Generative AI Tools" and "AI Educational Apps" had a significantly higher 
  rate of confirmed hallucination reports (40.0% of analyzed subset) than General 
  Chatbots (30.0%), showing that users are far more critical of factuality when 
  using AI for education and content creation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE USER-DERIVED TAXONOMY: 7 TYPES OF HALLUCINATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The paper outlines a hierarchy of how end-users perceive and describe AI lies:

1. H1: Factual Incorrectness (38% of reports) — Verifiably false info.
   - User Description: "The AI travel guide said Paris is the capital of England."
2. H3: Nonsensical/Irrelevant Output (25% of reports) — Grammatically correct 
   but semantically meaningless or off-topic responses.
   - User Description: "I asked for a sci-fi plot and it gave me a list of farm animals."
3. H2: Fabricated Information/Invention (15% of reports) — Active invention of 
   fictional sources, details, or features.
   - User Description: "My AI recipe app invented a spice called 'solar-salt'."
4. H4: Logical Inconsistency/Self-Contradiction — Inconsistent facts within a 
   conversation.
   - User Description: "First it said Saturday, then it insisted Tuesday."
5. H5: Persona Deviation/Role Inconsistency — Jarring tone or language shifts.
   - User Description: "Professional assistant suddenly used casual slang and emojis."
6. H6: Visual Fabrication (Generative AI only) — Physically impossible geometry.
   - User Description: "The avatar creator gave my character three hands."
7. H7: Repetitive Output (Non-functional) — Model loops or freezes.
   - User Description: "Got confused and kept typing 'hello' endlessly."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. LINGUISTIC SIGNATURES & SENTIMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* VADER Sentiment Score:
  - General non-hallucinating AI reviews: +0.15 (positive).
  - Overall reviews containing a hallucination: -0.40 (negative).
  - Review snippets describing the hallucination: -0.65 (highly negative).
* Distinctive N-grams: 
  Phrases like "wrong information," "made this up," "no sense at all," and 
  "completely made up" serve as statistical indicators of user frustration.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PRESENTATION OUTLINE TAKEAWAYS (STORYLINE C METAPHOR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Metaphor: "The Broken Mirror."
* Main Lesson: The final collapse of truth is marked by the user's sudden realization 
  that "My AI is Lying to Me." The illusion of a helpful, all-knowing companion 
  shatters, dropping user trust from a 3.9-star expectation to a 1.8-star reality.
* Slide Visual Concept: A cracked glass phone screen displaying an AI chat bubble 
  that says "Trust me, I know everything" while the glass fractures run through it.
* Headline: "My AI is Lying to Me: The 1.8-Star Reality of Synthesized Information."
