--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. INTRODUCTION & CORE COMMETARY THESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Recent philosophical/management work (e.g., Bieber & Unruh, 2025, "artificial 
  colleagues") analyzes AI systems as literal co-workers, assuming their adoption 
  in organizations is driven by practical, functional integration and task efficiency.
* Branda's Commentary: This functionalist view misses the mark. AI adoption is 
  fundamentally performative and symbolic.
* Core Hypothesis: "AI-first" strategies in modern enterprises are not objective 
  choices based on technology's actual utility. Instead, they are performative 
  acts of organizational rationality—symbolic actions taken to satisfy public, 
  investor, and institutional expectations of innovation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. KEY CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The paper develops three critical concepts for understanding the AI hype cycle:

A. PERFORMATIVE ORGANIZATIONAL RATIONALITY
* Definition: The practice of organizations adopting technologies not because they 
  solve an internal operational problem, but to signal to the market that the 
  organization is "rational," modern, and aligned with cutting-edge trends.
* Mechanism: The adoption acts as a badge of forward-thinking competency. By declaring 
  an "AI-first" mandate, management secures legitimacy, maintains stock value, and 
  conforms to peer pressure, regardless of whether the tools actually function or 
  are used.

B. THE PERFORMATIVE TRANSLATION OF AI HYPE
* Definition: The process by which inflated marketing promises (hype) from model 
  creators are translated into hard internal mandates by corporate leaders.
* Impact: Hype is not just passive noise; it is an active, performative force. 
  It creates an artificial necessity to adopt AI, forcing departments to deploy 
  models to fit the narrative, even if human workflows are disrupted or deskilled.

C. THE NARRATIVE-PRACTICE GAP
* Definition: The discrepancy between a company's external marketing narrative 
  ("we are powered by state-of-the-art AI-first systems") and its internal practical 
  reality (basic API wrappers, underutilized features, or manual human labor 
  shadowing the AI's mistakes).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. IMPLICATIONS FOR THE SLIDE STORYLINE (STORYLINE C: THE EROSION OF TRUTH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Metaphor: "The Performative Shield."
* Role in the Story: This paper represents the Genesis—the first domino. Before 
  students/employees feel like imposters, and before LLMs start lying to them, 
  the organization itself starts with a performative lie: that it must be "AI-first."
* The Domino Effect:
  1. Hype (Performative "AI-first" mandates) -> 
  2. Forced Adoption (Cheatbots/academic shortcuts to meet output targets) -> 
  3. Identity Crisis (AI Imposter syndrome due to lack of ownership) -> 
  4. Grief/Escape (AI Resurrection as humans retreat into digital ghosts) -> 
  5. Collapse (Total delusion as users trust models that lie to them).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Slide Visual Concept: A corporate boardroom where the PowerPoint displays a
