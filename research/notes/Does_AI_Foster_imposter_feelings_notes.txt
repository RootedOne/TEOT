  PAPER: Does AI Foster Imposter Feelings? The Impact of Task Design on Students' Use of AI

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ABSTRACT & CORE HYPOTHESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The rapid growth of generative AI in higher education allows students to complete 
  complex academic tasks in seconds, offering polished and high-quality outputs.
* However, tasks designed by educators have not evolved alongside these tools.
* Core Dilemma: When students use AI as a complete replacement for their learning 
  process rather than an aid, their overall cognitive growth and psychological 
  well-being are severely impacted.
* Hypothesis: The psychological impact of AI on students is mediated by task design.
  Specifically, "Delegable Tasks" (which allow students to offload the core cognitive 
  work to AI) reduce psychological ownership, lower perceived effort, erode agency, 
  and ultimately foster and intensify Academic Impostor Syndrome (AIS) over time.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METHODOLOGY: EXPERIMENTAL SETUP & TASK DESIGN METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The authors conducted three empirical studies with social sciences students, utilizing 
an experimental framework to test how different task designs shape the AI-human 
interaction loop.

The tasks were categorized into two distinct paradigms (using a case study on IKEA's 
second-hand furniture marketplace, "IKEA Preowned"):

A. DELEGABLE TASKS (Offloading Paradigm)
* Definition: Tasks where the primary cognitive requirements are information retrieval, 
  summarization, structural comparisons, and basic profiling. These tasks are easily 
  solved by AI with simple copy-paste prompts, requiring minimal human intervention.
* IKEA Case Study Examples:
  1. Summarizing IKEA's differentiation strategies in the second-hand market.
  2. Formatting a comparison matrix of business models (IKEA vs. Wallapop/Vinted).
  3. Describing how sustainability concerns impact IKEA's youth brand perception.
  4. Outlining a demographic consumer profile for re-commerce buyers.
  5. Comparing pricing structures numerically between primary and secondary markets.
* Cognitive Outcome: Simple information retrieval and structured comparison. 
  Students act as "postmen" or "editors" rather than creators.

B. SUPPORTED TASKS (Co-Creation/Synthesis Paradigm)
* Definition: Tasks requiring strategic evaluation, high-level judgment, cultural 
  interpretation, experimental design critique, and decoding anomaly behaviors. 
  AI cannot solve these tasks independently; it can only support the student, 
  who must guide, synthesize, and make final value judgments.
* IKEA Case Study Examples:
  1. Formulating strategic recommendations on how IKEA can compete against 
     Wallapop without diluting its premium brand identity.
  2. Interpreting the complex cultural influence of the "Greta effect" on global 
     consumer psychology and sales.
  3. Proposing mechanisms for adapting to re-commerce trends without alienating 
     loyal, legacy customers.
  4. Critically evaluating an experimental pilot program (IKEA Preowned) based 
     on experimental design, variables, and data collection.
  5. Explaining anomalous consumer behaviors (e.g., why buying used goods on 
     Wallapop is sometimes more expensive than buying brand-new items from IKEA).
* Cognitive Outcome: Critical reasoning, value judgment, and strategic synthesis. 
  The human remains the pilot; the AI serves as the radar.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE PSYCHOLOGICAL METRICS MEASURED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The studies tracked five critical psychological variables:

1. Perceived Learning: The student’s conscious estimation of new knowledge acquired.
2. Perceived Effort: The mental energy expended in task resolution.
3. Student Agency (Sense of Agency - SoA): The degree of control students feel they 
   have over their own thoughts, decisions, and output.
4. Psychological Ownership (PO): The possessive feeling that "this work belongs 
   to me" because I invested my personal energy, time, and intellect into it.
5. Academic Impostor Syndrome (AIS): The persistent, internal feeling of being 
   an intellectual fraud, fearing that others will realize they are incompetent 
   and rely entirely on external crutches (the AI) to succeed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. KEY FINDINGS: THE DELEGATION SPIRAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Delegation Trap: Tasks that permit or encourage students to delegate 
  decision-making/work to AI degrade the overall learning experience. Students 
  expend less effort, resulting in a drop in perceived learning.
* The Eeriness of Zero-Effort: When students get a perfect paper with zero mental 
  exertion, they fail to develop Psychological Ownership. They view the output 
  as the AI's product, not their own.
* The Impostor Incubator: The mismatch between the high quality of the AI-generated 
  output and the low effort input by the student creates a profound sense of 
  fraudulence.
* The Sleeper Effect: Crucially, these imposter feelings do not fade as students 
  become more familiar with AI. Instead, the study reveals a temporal "sleeper 
  effect"—the feelings of being a fake intensify over time as dependency grows.
* Supported Tasks as a Shield: When tasks are designed to be "supported" (requiring 
  strategic and subjective human input), AI usage does not foster imposter 
  syndrome. Because the student must direct and filter the AI, their sense of 
  agency and psychological ownership remains intact.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PRESENTATION OUTLINE TAKEAWAYS (STORYLINE C METAPHOR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Metaphor: "The Insecurity of the Shadow Author."
* Main Lesson: If we design systems and roles where humans only copy-paste and 
  delegate, we cultivate a generation of insecure, imposter-ridden professionals.
* Slide Visual Concept: A student standing before an enormous, flawless academic 
  monument, holding a tiny remote control, looking terrified.
* Headline: "Who wrote this? The rise of the human ghostwriter."
