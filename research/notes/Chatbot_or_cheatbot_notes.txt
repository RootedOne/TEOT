  EXECUTIVE ANALYSIS & SYSTEMATIC NOTES
  PAPER: Chatbot or cheatbot? Moral judgments of AI usage in academic writing
  AUTHORS: Yi Xu, Junyi Guo, Dong Zhou & Mengxuan Li
  PUBLICATION: Education and Information Technologies (March 2026)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ABSTRACT & CORE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Context: Generative AI tools (LLMs) are widely integrated into academic and 
  professional writing, causing ethical friction.
* The Problem: The emergence of "AI-giarism"—a new form of academic/intellectual 
  misconduct where AI-generated content blurs the lines of traditional plagiarism.
* Objective: The study examines how moral judgments differ based on human effort 
  and context, mapping the cognitive and emotional pathways that shape how users 
  rationalize cheating.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METHODOLOGY & THE THREE SCENARIOS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Sample: N = 415 participants evaluated under a questionnaire design based on 
  dual-process moral reasoning models.
* Scenarios Evaluated:
  1. Direct Copying (The Offloading Trap): 
     Submitting fully or nearly fully AI-generated content as one's own without 
     significant human modification and without disclosure.
     - Moral Judgment: Strictest moral condemnation. Viewed as outright theft/plagiarism.
  2. Collaborative Writing (The Co-Creation Loop): 
     Using AI as an active partner to brainstorm, structure outlines, draft 
     sections, or iterate content.
     - Moral Judgment: Moderate ethical evaluation. Viewed as a gray area with 
       varying levels of acceptable collaboration.
  3. Minimal Tool Use (The Editing Shield): 
     Using AI exclusively for grammar checks, proofreading, clarity, translation, 
     or basic search assistance.
     - Moral Judgment: Ethically ambiguous, but generally tolerated or viewed 
       as a standard utility.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE PSYCHOLOGICAL PATHWAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The authors identify key psychological variables that determine moral judgments:

* Perceived Moral Intensity (Core Mediator): 
  Perceived moral intensity determines how "wrong" a task feels. How deeply a 
  person understands plagiarism standards (cognitive awareness) and their 
  negative emotional reaction to cheating (moral disgust) drive this intensity.
* The AI Experience Moderator (AI Literacy):
  - Highly Experienced AI Users: Exhibit sophisticated, nuanced ethical 
    judgments, distinguishing clearly between editing, collaboration, and plagiarism.
  - Novices: Show simple, black-and-white evaluations (either classifying all AI 
    use as cheating, or blindly offloading work without understanding the moral cost).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. IMPLICATIONS FOR THE SLIDE STORYLINE (STORYLINE C: THE EROSION OF TRUTH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Metaphor: "The Silent Deal."
* Role in the Story: Slide 2 (The Shortcut / Cheatbot Phase).
* The Connection: 
  - Slide 1 (boardroom hype) sets up performative "AI-first" demands.
  - Slide 2 (this paper) shows how humans respond to this pressure: we strike a 
    silent deal with the machine. We use it to cut corners, rationalizing "AI-giarism" 
    along a spectrum from minimal checks to direct copying.
  - This shortcut leads to Slide 3 (imposter feelings), because offloading the 
    effort erodes our sense of authorship and psychological ownership.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PRESENTATION OUTLINE TAKEAWAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Headline: "Chatbot or Cheatbot? The Spectrum of AI-Giarism."
* Slide Visual Concept: A scale where on one side sits a human hand holding a pen 
  labeled "Original Effort," and on the other sits a robot arm typing labeled 
  "Zero Effort." The scale is tipped, showing the erosion of moral boundaries.
