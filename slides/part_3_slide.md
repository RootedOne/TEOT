## 🖥️ Slide Content

### **THE CRISIS HOOK**
> *"If the machine does all the thinking, are you still the author of your own success?"*

### **THE ANATOMY OF ETHICAL FRICTION**
*   **The Delegation Trap:** When task designs allow us to delegate the core cognitive work to AI, we sacrifice our learning and effort.
*   **Diluted Agency & Ownership:** Without investing personal mental energy, we fail to develop *Psychological Ownership* ("This work is mine"). The output belongs to the machine.
*   **The Sleeper Effect:** Imposter feelings do not fade as we become more familiar with AI. Instead, they intensify over time, creating a chronic state of perceived fraudulence. *(Batista-Toledo & Gavilan, 2026)*

## 🎙️ Speaker Notes (Ali's Script)
*"You got the report done. It's flawless. Your boss or teacher is impressed. But inside, you feel a cold knot of panic. You feel like a fake. You didn't write it. You didn't even think it. You just prompted it.*

*This is the psychological tax of the shortcut.*

*Santiago Batista-Toledo and Diana Gavilan’s 2026 study reveals a direct causal loop. When tasks are designed to be 'delegable'—simple summaries, info retrieval, structured data copying—we offload the brainwork. And when we offload the work, we destroy our Sense of Agency and Psychological Ownership. We can no longer look at the work and say, 'I did this.'*

*And here is the kicker: the 'Sleeper Effect.' Many technologists tell you that as we get used to AI, this discomfort will fade. The science says the exact opposite. The feelings of fraudulence intensify over time. The better the AI gets, and the more we rely on it, the more we feel like imposters in our own careers.*

*We are deskilling ourselves, living in the shadow of our own tools. And when our creative reality becomes this hollow, when our sense of self is eroded at work... we look for an escape. We turn to the digital world not to write papers, but to reclaim our humanity—even if it means trying to resurrect the dead."*
