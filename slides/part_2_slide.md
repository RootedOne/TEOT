## 🖥️ Slide Content

### **THE SHORTCUT HOOK**
> *"When keeping up requires letting go, where do you draw the line between a helpful chatbot and a cheatbot?"*

### **THE SPEC-SHEET: THE SPECTRUM OF AI-GIARISM**
*   **Direct Copying:** Pure delegation. Copying raw AI outputs directly without modification or disclosure. *(Strictly Condemned)*
*   **Collaborative Writing:** The hybrid partner. AI outlines, drafts chunks, and prompts human thought. *(The Gray Zone)*
*   **Minimal Tool Use:** The editor. Grammar checking, translation, and text-polishing. *(Ethically Ambiguous but Tolerated)*

### **THE CORE PSYCHOLOGY**
*   **Perceived Moral Intensity:** Our ethical boundary is driven by how "wrong" a shortcut feels.
*   **The Literacy Shield:** The more experienced you are with AI, the more sophisticated your ethical boundaries become. Novices see it as black-and-white (pure threat or total savior), leading to reckless offloading. *(Xu et al., 2026)*

## 🎙️ Speaker Notes (Ali's Script)
*"So, management announced the 'AI-First' mandate. The metrics are higher, the speed is faster, and the pressure is real. To survive, you have to hit targets that feel humanly impossible.*

*So you strike a deal.*

*You open up a chatbot. At first, it’s just for grammar. Then it’s for drafting an outline. Then, on a late Tuesday night with a deadline staring you down, you copy-paste the model's output directly. You’ve crossed into the territory of 'AI-giarism.'*

*Yi Xu's 2026 study in 'Education and Information Technologies' shows that we rationalize this through a spectrum. We convince ourselves that using a 'cheatbot' is just standard collaboration. But there’s a trap here: our moral judgment depends on our experience. Novices who don't understand how these models work treat them as magic boxes—offloading entire chunks of cognitive work without a second thought.*

*But what happens when you hit 'submit' on a perfect document you didn't write? You get the credit, you get the grade, you get the promotion.* 

*But you also get a lingering, quiet voice in the back of your head. And that voice is about to drive you into an identity crisis: Imposter Syndrome."*
