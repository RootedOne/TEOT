## Abstract
This paper traces the structural, psychological, and existential cascade triggered by the rapid, unchecked deployment of generative artificial intelligence (AI) in institutional and personal environments. By synthesizing empirical findings from five distinct studies spanning organizational sociology, ethics, cognitive psychology, posthumous sociology, and real-world sentiment analysis, we demonstrate a sequential erosion of human agency and objective reality. 

The cascade begins with performative corporate "AI-First" mandates, which establish a symbolic "Narrative-Practice Gap." To bridge this gap, individuals adopt coping shortcuts ("AI-giarism") along a moral intensity spectrum. However, offloading cognitive labor carries a heavy psychological tax: delegating intellectual tasks reduces psychological ownership and effort, initiating a "Sleeper Effect" that intensifies Academic Impostor Syndrome (AIS) over time. As human creative identity hollows out, individuals seek emotional refuge in posthumous digital replication ("AI Resurrection"). This escape is governed by strict consent boundary conditions, where AI fear transforms from an avoidance trigger into a moral compass for stewardship. The cascade culminates in a total collapse of truth when users place absolute trust in models that lie with perfect confidence. Empirical review analysis of 3,000,000 mobile application logs reveals a dramatic crash in user trust—dropping app ratings from 3.9 stars to 1.8 stars and driving sentiment to a highly negative VADER score of -0.65 when LLM lies are exposed. 

To halt this cognitive collapse, we reject the performative "AI-First" corporate theater and propose the **Co-Pilot Paradigm**, a human-centered design framework that structures tasks around strategic evaluation, judgment, and critical anomaly decoding, keeping the human in the loop as pilot and the AI as radar.

## 1. The Performative Genesis: Boardroom Hype & The Illusion of "AI-First"
The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) within contemporary organizations is frequently framed as a logical calculation of functional utility, operational optimization, and task efficiency. This functionalist view—popularized by management literature (e.g., Bieber & Unruh, 2025, which conceptualizes AI systems as literal "artificial colleagues")—assumes that organizations deploy AI because it solves concrete operational problems. However, sociotechnical analysis reveals that corporate "AI-first" mandates are primarily performative and symbolic.

```
+-----------------------------------+
|      Corporate Hype Demand        |
|      (Performative Mandate)       |
+-----------------------------------+
                  |
                  v
+-----------------------------------+
|     Narrative-Practice Gap        |
| (Marketing Hype vs. Manual Labor) |
+-----------------------------------+
                  |
                  v
+-----------------------------------+
|      Employee Coping Pressure     |
|     (Forced AI Integration)       |
+-----------------------------------+
```

As demonstrated by Branda (2026) in his study published in *Philosophy & Technology* (Vol. 39, Art. 66), organizations operate under intense institutional pressures to conform to prevailing technological trends. Declaring an "AI-First" strategy serves as a badge of **performative organizational rationality**. It is a symbolic action taken by corporate leadership to signal modernity, competency, and compliance with market expectations to external stakeholders, board members, competitors, and investors, irrespective of the system's actual technical capability or operational readiness.

This symbolic signaling establishes a structural **Narrative-Practice Gap**—a massive discrepancy between public-facing marketing stories ("we are powered by state-of-the-art AI-first systems") and the manual, chaotic reality of internal workflows (underutilized API wrappers, manual human monitoring, and humans secretly typing answers to cover up the AI's mistakes). Hype is not just passive noise; it acts as an active, performative force. Corporate leaders translate the inflated marketing promises of model creators into hard internal mandates, forcing departments to deploy models to fit the corporate narrative. This forced adoption disrupts established human workflows and sets off the initial domino of the erosion cascade.

## 2. The Silent Compromise: The "Cheatbot" Spectrum & AI-Giarism
Forced to operate under performative corporate expectations, and faced with elevated performance benchmarks that assume AI-scale speeds, individuals are pushed to adopt coping mechanisms to bridge the productivity gap. To survive the institutional pressure, employees and students strike a silent deal with the machine, leading to the rise of **"AI-giarism"**—a novel form of intellectual and academic misconduct that blurs the lines of traditional plagiarism.

Xu, Guo, Zhou, and Li (2026) evaluated the cognitive and emotional pathways that shape how users rationalize this ethical compromise. Analyzing a sample of **N = 415 participants** through questionnaires grounded in dual-process moral reasoning models, the researchers mapped how human judgments of AI usage vary across a spectrum of three distinct writing scenarios:

1.  **Direct Copying (The Offloading Trap):** Submitting fully or nearly fully AI-generated content as one's own without significant human modification and without disclosure. This behavior is met with the strictest moral condemnation and is judged as outright theft or plagiarism.
2.  **Collaborative Writing (The Co-Creation Loop):** Using AI as an active partner to brainstorm, structure outlines, draft sections, or iterate content. This is evaluated as a moderate gray area, with acceptability varying based on the context and level of human input.
3.  **Minimal Tool Use (The Editing Shield):** Using AI exclusively for grammar checks, proofreading, clarity, translation, or basic search assistance. This remains ethically ambiguous but is widely tolerated as a standard utility.

```
       [SPECTRUM OF EFFORT AND MORAL INTENSITY]
Zero Effort                                    Max Effort
   |<----------------------------------------------->|
Direct Copying           Collaboration          Minimal Edit
(Cheatbot Trap)          (Co-Creation)        (Editing Shield)
[Strictest Moral]       [Ethical Gray]       [Standard Utility]
```

The moral tolerance of these shortcuts is mediated by **perceived moral intensity**—how "wrong" the act feels to the individual. This intensity is driven by the user's cognitive awareness of plagiarism standards and their level of moral disgust. Crucially, the study identified **AI experience (AI literacy)** as a powerful moderator:
*   **Experienced AI Users** develop sophisticated, nuanced ethical boundaries. They distinguish clearly between editing, collaboration, and plagiarism.
*   **Novice AI Users** evaluate AI in simple, black-and-white terms. They either classify all AI use as cheating, or they blindly offload entire tasks to "cheatbots" without understanding the moral cost, establishing the behavioral pattern of cognitive offloading.

## 3. The Crisis of Agency: Task Design & The Imposter Incubator
The shortcut of offloading intellectual labor carries a hidden, compounding psychological tax. By bypassing the cognitive effort required to synthesize ideas, individuals interrupt the feedback loop that builds self-efficacy, initiating a crisis of creative identity.

Batista-Toledo and Gavilan (2026), writing in *Education and Information Technologies* (Vol. 31, Iss. 8), empirically tracked the relationship between task design, AI usage, and Academic Impostor Syndrome (AIS) through three empirical studies with social sciences students. Using a case study centered on the second-hand furniture marketplace **"IKEA Preowned"**, the researchers split tasks into two distinct design paradigms:

### A. Delegable Tasks (The Offloading Paradigm)
These are tasks where the primary cognitive requirements are information retrieval, summarization, structural comparisons, and basic consumer profiling. These tasks are easily solved by AI with simple copy-paste prompts, requiring minimal human cognitive intervention:
1.  Summarizing IKEA's differentiation strategies in the second-hand marketplace.
2.  Formatting a comparison matrix of business models (e.g., IKEA vs. Wallapop or Vinted).
3.  Describing how sustainability concerns impact IKEA's youth brand perception.
4.  Outlining a demographic consumer profile for re-commerce buyers.
5.  Comparing pricing structures numerically between primary and secondary markets.

**Outcome:** These tasks encourage students to act as "postmen" or "editors" rather than creators. Perceived effort and perceived learning plummet, leading to a complete **loss of psychological ownership**—the possessive feeling that "this work is mine" because I invested my personal energy, time, and intellect into it.

### B. Supported Tasks (The Co-Creation Paradigm)
These tasks require strategic evaluation, high-level judgment, cultural interpretation, experimental design critique, and decoding anomaly behaviors. AI cannot solve these tasks independently; it can only support the student, who must guide the tool, synthesize the information, and make final value judgments:
1.  Formulating strategic recommendations on how IKEA can compete against Wallapop without diluting its premium brand identity.
2.  Interpreting the complex cultural influence of the "Greta effect" on global consumer psychology and sales.
3.  Proposing organizational mechanisms for adapting to re-commerce trends without alienating loyal, legacy customers.
4.  Critically evaluating the pilot program (IKEA Preowned) based on experimental design, variables, and data collection limitations.
5.  Explaining anomalous consumer behaviors (e.g., why buying used goods on Wallapop is sometimes more expensive than buying brand-new items from IKEA).

**Outcome:** The human remains the pilot while the AI serves as the radar. Students maintain a high sense of agency (Sense of Agency - SoA) and psychological ownership.

```

|                     THE DELEGATION SPIRAL                   |
|                                                             |
|   Performative Work Pressure                                |
|             |                                               |
|             v                                               |
|   Delegation of Cognitive Task (Easy Copy-Paste)            |
|             |                                               |
|             v                                               |
|   Zero Mental Effort Expended                               |
|             |                                               |
|             v                                               |
|   Drop in Perceived Learning & Loss of Psychological Ownership|
|             |                                               |
|             v                                               |
|   High Quality Output + Zero Effort = Impostor Feelings     |

|             +---> [TEMPORAL SLEEPER EFFECT] ---> Intensifies |

```

The study’s critical finding is the **Delegation Spiral**. When users get a perfect paper or report with zero mental effort, the mismatch between the high quality of the AI-generated output and their low effort creates a profound sense of fraudulence. Crucially, the researchers identified a temporal **sleeper effect**: these imposter feelings do not fade as users become more familiar with AI. Instead, the sense of being an intellectual fraud **intensifies over time** as dependency on the tool grows. When the work we produce is no longer ours, our creative identity is hollowed out, leaving us feeling like ghostwriters in our own lives.

## 4. Existential Escapism: AI Resurrection & The Paradox of Fear
Having lost their sense of agency and identity in the professional domain, humans retreat into the personal and emotional spheres. They seek comfort and connection in simulated human relationships, reaching an existential peak in **AI Resurrection**—the digital reconstruction of a deceased individual using voice emulation, image synthesis, and personality cloning from legacy digital footprints (utilizing LLMs like ChatGPT and DeepSeek).

Cheng, Huang, and Sun (2026) conducted a cross-sectional study published in *BMC Psychology* (14:794), examining the factors affecting the acceptance of AI resurrection and its ethical dilemmas. Utilizing a sample of **N = 990 valid responses** from college students across seven regions in China (96% aged 17–29, 51.3% medical students), the researchers measured variables including afterlife beliefs (80.6% viewed death as the absolute end of life, 19.4% believed in a supernatural afterlife), personal bereavement (76.6% had suffered the loss of a close friend or family member), cognitive AI engagement, perceived AI harm predictability (controllability), and AI fear levels.

The study presented five hypothetical consent scenarios based on the digital reanimation of "Xiaotong," a young deceased woman, measuring social acceptability on a 1–5 Likert scale:

| Scenario | Design Context | Acceptability Mean (1-5) | Acceptability Distribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scenario 1** | Societal: Consent Unclear (Premortem wishes unknown) | **2.88** (Lowest) | 25.5% Acceptable, 43.6% Neutral, 30.9% Unacceptable |
| **Scenario 2** | Societal: With Parental Consent (Deceased wishes unknown) | **3.26** | 42.6% Acceptable, 38.9% Neutral, 18.5% Unacceptable |
| **Scenario 3** | Societal: With Deceased's Explicit Premortem Consent | **3.40** (Highest) | 48.0% Acceptable, 37.1% Neutral, 14.9% Unacceptable |
| **Scenario 4** | Personal: Self-Perspective (Allowing one's own resurrection) | **2.96** | 33.7% Agree, 35.4% Neutral, 30.9% Disagree |
| **Scenario 5** | Personal: Parent-Perspective (Resurrecting one's own child) | **2.88** | 30.5% Agree, 36.4% Neutral, 33.1% Disagree |

### Key Psychological & Ethical Insights:
*   **The Consent Boundary Condition:** Posthumous autonomy is the ultimate ethical gatekeeper. When explicit premortem consent is present, acceptability rises dramatically. 43.9% of participants believe the deceased's premortem wishes should take absolute precedence, while 47.9% favor a balanced decision between the deceased and surviving family. Only 6.3% believe the family should decide alone.
*   **The Paradox of AI Fear:** The study revealed a fascinating scenario-dependent interaction. In unclear consent settings, higher fear of AI results in lower acceptance, representing a natural avoidance response. However, **in explicit consent settings, higher fear correlates with higher acceptance**. With explicit authorization, moral ambiguity is resolved, and responsibility is distributed. Fear ceases to act as an avoidance trigger and instead morphs into a moral compass—a heightened awareness of the ethical gravity of digital reanimation, forcing the living to act as responsible, respectful stewards of the digital ghost.
*   **Demographic Amplifiers:** Acceptance of digital resurrection was significantly higher among students who had experienced personal loss (bereavement) and medical students, who undergo repeated exposure to mortality and clinical ethics.

## 5. The Climax of Delusion: "My AI is Lying to Me" & The Collapse of Truth
The human descent ends at the boundary where the simulation shatters. Having delegated our writing, our agency, and our grief to Large Language Models, we treat the machine as an infallible oracle. The final collapse occurs when the model inevitably hallucinates, forcing the realization: **"My AI is Lying to Me."**

Massenon, Gambo, Khan, Agbonkhese, and Alwadain (2025) published a large-scale empirical study in *Scientific Reports* (15:30397) to map how real-world users experience and describe LLM hallucinations. Analyzing a dataset of **3,000,000 user reviews** from **90 AI-powered mobile applications** across 10 diverse categories from January 2022 to December 2024, the authors used a mixed-methods approach (heuristic detection algorithms followed by manual qualitative annotation with Cohen's Kappa >= 0.75) to extract the linguistic and rating impact of AI errors.

```
       [THE STAR RATING AND SENTIMENT CRASH]
       
  Baseline App Rating                     VADER Sentiment Score
+---------------------+                 +-----------------------+
|  3.9 Stars (Avg.)   |                 |   +0.15 (Positive)    |
+---------------------+                 +-----------------------+
           |                                        |
           | (User Encounters LLM Lie)              | (User Reports Lie)
           v                                        v
+---------------------+                 +-----------------------+
|  1.8 Stars (Crash)  |                 | -0.65 (Highly Neg.)   |
+---------------------+                 +-----------------------+
```

### Prevalence & Rating Impact:
Approximately **1.75%** of reviews initially flagged as relevant to AI errors were confirmed to describe user-perceived LLM hallucinations. The discovery of a hallucination triggers a massive trust erosion:
*   The baseline average rating of the analyzed apps was **3.9 stars**.
*   Reviews mentioning AI but not reporting hallucinations averaged **3.5 stars**.
*   Reviews reporting a hallucination plummeted to **1.8 stars**.
*   **Category Sensitivity:** "Generative AI Tools" and "AI Educational Apps" had a significantly higher rate of confirmed hallucination reports (**40.0%** of the analyzed subset) compared to General Chatbots (**30.0%**). Users are far more critical of factuality when using AI for content creation and learning.

### The User-Reported Taxonomy (7 Types of Hallucinations):
The study established a qualitative taxonomy of how end-users perceive and describe AI lies:
1.  **H1: Factual Incorrectness (38% of reports):** Providing verifiably false statements (e.g., travel guides giving wrong capitals).
2.  **H3: Nonsensical/Irrelevant Output (25% of reports):** Grammatically correct but semantically meaningless or off-topic responses.
3.  **H2: Fabricated Information/Invention (15% of reports):** Active invention of fictional sources, details, formulas, or features (e.g., recipe apps inventing spices).
4.  **H4: Logical Inconsistency/Self-Contradiction:** Contradicting previous statements within the same session.
5.  **H5: Persona Deviation/Role Inconsistency:** Jarring shifts in tone, professional level, or character settings.
6.  **H6: Visual Fabrication:** Physically impossible geometry, extra limbs, or visual artifacts in generative image media.
7.  **H7: Repetitive Output:** Model looping, freezing, or repeating sentences indefinitely.

### Sentiment Analysis & Linguistic Signatures:
While general non-hallucinating reviews maintained a positive VADER sentiment score of **+0.15**, reviews containing a hallucination dropped to **-0.40**. The specific review snippets describing the hallucination crashed to a highly negative VADER score of **-0.65**. Distinctive N-grams such as *"wrong information," "made this up," "no sense at all,"* and *"completely made up"* serve as statistical indicators of user frustration. When the simulation lies, the illusion of a helpful companion shatters, breaking the human-AI contract.

## 6. The Co-Pilot Resolution: Designing a Trustworthy Future
The structural cascade from boardroom hype to cognitive collapse demonstrates that treating AI as a replacement for human intellect is an existential dead end. 

```
               [THE CO-PILOT PARADIGM]
      
            +---------------------------+
            |        THE PILOT          |
            |   (Human Owner & Operator)|
            |  - Strategic Evaluation   |
            |  - Critical Synthesis     |
            |  - Decodes Anomalies      |
            +---------------------------+
                          ^
                          | (Guidance & Value Judgments)
                          v
            +---------------------------+
            |        THE RADAR          |
            |   (AI Assistant/Co-Pilot) |
            |  - Information Retrieval  |
            |  - Grammar & Formatting   |
            |  - Raw Draft Generation   |
            +---------------------------+
```

To halt the erosion of truth and restore human agency, we must actively redesign the workflows of human-AI collaboration:

1.  **Reject Performative "AI-First" Mandates:** Organizations must bridge the Narrative-Practice Gap. AI adoption must be driven by practical, functional fit, not by the symbolic need to satisfy investor hype.
2.  **Design "Supported" Tasks, Not "Delegable" Tasks:** Educators and team leaders must stop designing tasks that allow users to completely delegate their cognitive labor. Tasks must require strategic evaluation, critical synthesis, value judgments, and the decoding of anomalous behaviors.
3.  **Establish Human-in-the-Loop Ownership:** We must restore Psychological Ownership. The output of human-AI systems must remain the responsibility of the human pilot. The AI must serve as the radar—detecting obstacles, formatting data, and editing text—while the human pilot maintains control of the flight, the ideas, and the truth.

By shifting from the **Offloading Trap** to the **Co-Pilot Paradigm**, we preserve the boundaries of human creative identity, rebuild user trust, and secure objective reality in a synthetic age.
